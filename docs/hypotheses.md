# Hypotheses and planned comparisons

The workspace contains several evolving protocols. These are bounded hypotheses, not findings or a retroactive preregistration:

- Outcome definitions and anchoring may change estimated progression prevalence, discrimination and calibration.
- Adding modalities may change probabilistic accuracy on matched participants; phase/coverage selection and missingness can confound apparent gains.
- Temporal information may add value over appropriately matched history-summary and latest-assessment comparators.
- Diagnosis-linked inputs may account for apparent model value, motivating separately versioned refitted exclusions.
- Onset-defined subgroup calibration may differ from enrollment-age subgroup calibration, conditional on feasible authorized NACC data.

Retain a simple penalised-logistic primary comparison unless a justified, dated protocol establishes another selection rule. Separate exploratory model screening from confirmatory contrasts. Do not apply thresholds or hypotheses selected after seeing outputs as if they were prospectively fixed. Fresh confirmation and multiplicity decisions remain unresolved for stronger claims.
